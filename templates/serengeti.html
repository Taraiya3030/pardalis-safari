Section 1: Ecosystem & Biodiversity

Covering over 14,750 square kilometers (5,700 square miles), Serengeti National Park derives its name from the Maasai word 'Siringet', meaning "the land that moves on forever." Established in 1951 and designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981, it hosts the most complex and intact wildlife ecosystem remaining on Earth.

The park is divided into three major geographical sectors:

Central Serengeti (Seronera Valley)

Characterized by open acacia savannas, granite kopjes (rock outcrops), and permanent river networks. Seronera is world-famous for density of big cats—particularly leopards, lions, and cheetahs.

Western Corridor (Grumeti River Region)

Dominated by riverine forests and floodplains. Home to massive Nile crocodiles and hippos along the treacherous Grumeti River crossings during May and June.

Northern Serengeti (Mara River Region)

Hilly grasslands bordering Kenya's Maasai Mara. This region features dramatic Mara River crossings between July and October as herds search for fresh pastures.

Section 2: The Great Wildebeest Migration

The Great Migration is widely regarded as one of the Seven Natural Wonders of Africa. Over 1.5 million wildebeest, 250,000 zebras, and hundreds of thousands of gazelles move in an endless 1,000-kilometer circular trek driven by seasonal rainfall patterns.

Seasonal Migration Calendar

January – March (Southern Serengeti / Ndutu): Calving season where over 8000 wildebeest calves are born daily, drawing intense predator action.
April – May (Central & Western Serengeti): Herds move north through Seronera toward the Western Corridor as the rains finish.
June – July (Grumeti River): The herds encounter their first treacherous river obstacle, facing large crocodiles.
August – October (Northern Serengeti & Mara River): The iconic dramatic river crossings into Kenya, featuring high-stakes predator encounters.
November – December (Return South): The herds march back south along the eastern boundaries to repeat the cycle.

Section 3: Wildlife Safaris & Maasai Culture

A safari in the Serengeti offers unprecedented opportunities to view Africa's iconic "Big Five" (Lion, Leopard, Elephant, Buffalo, and Rhinoceros) in their natural habitat.

Big Cat & Wildlife Viewing

With more than 3,000 lions, high cheetah populations on open plains, and leopards stealthily hunting from acacia branches, predator sightings are a daily occurrence on our game drives.

Maasai Cultural Visits

Engage with the indigenous Maasai people who have coexisted with Serengeti wildlife for centuries. Learn about traditional customs, beadwork crafts, and ancestral warrior dances during authentic village visits.

Section 4: Travel Essentials & Planning

Best Time to Visit

Dry Season (June to October): Best overall time for game viewing, crisp weather, and witnessing northern river crossings.
Green Season (November to May): Lush landscapes, excellent birdwatching (over 500 species), baby animals, and fewer crowds.

Getting There & Around

Fly into Kilimanjaro International Airport (JRO) or Arusha Airport (ARK), followed by a scenic 4x4 land safari or a quick bush flight directly into airstrips such as Seronera, Kogatende, or Grumeti.
