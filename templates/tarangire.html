Tarangire National Park

The Sanctuary of Elephants & Ancient Baobabs

Tarangire National Park is one of Tanzania’s most captivating safari destinations, covering approximately 2,850 square kilometers in the Manyara Region. Located just 120 kilometers southwest of Arusha City, Tarangire forms a critical seasonal sanctuary within the vast Maasai Steppe ecosystem. Established in 1970, the park derives its name from the life-sustaining Tarangire River, which flows directly through its core.

Famous worldwide for hosting some of the highest concentrations of African elephants on the continent, Tarangire features an extraordinary landscape dotted with towering, thousand-year-old Baobab trees. During the dry season, the park transforms into a bustling haven as thousands of migratory animals travel from dry surrounding steppes in search of water along the riverbed.

Unlike other northern circuit parks, Tarangire offers a quieter, intimate wilderness experience. Visitors encounter diverse habitats ranging from seasonal swamps and acacia woodlands to riverine forests and open savannah ridge tops.

Wildlife Found in Tarangire National Park

Tarangire supports a rich variety of big game and wildlife species. Its permanent water sources during the dry months draw immense numbers of animals from across the entire ecosystem.

African Elephants

Tarangire is world-renowned for its colossal elephant herds, sometimes numbering up to 300 individuals in a single group. Families, calves, and magnificent matriarchs can easily be seen drinking, bathing, and digging for fresh water in dry riverbeds.

Tree-Climbing Lions & Predators

The park is home to thriving lion prides that, similar to those in Lake Manyara, often climb baobab and acacia trees to escape the heat and insects. Elusive leopards, cheetahs, and spotted hyenas are also active hunters across the open savannahs.

Massive Migration Grazers

During the dry months (July to October), thousands of wildebeests, zebras, elands, and impalas gather around the Tarangire River. This seasonal gathering offers safari travelers game viewing second only to the Great Migration in the Serengeti.

Giraffes and Antelopes

Maasai giraffes wander gracefully among acacia trees, while rare and specialized antelope species roam the park plains:

  • Fringe-eared Oryx
  • Greater Kudu
  • Lesser Kudu
  • Waterbuck
  • Gerenuk
  • Thomson's Gazelle
  • Impala
  • Dik-Dik

Bird Watching Paradise

Boasting over 550 recorded avian species, Tarangire is an absolute haven for bird enthusiasts. Key sightings include:

  • Lilac-Breasted Roller
  • Yellow-Collared Lovebird
  • Ashy Starling
  • Kori Bustard
  • Ground Hornbill
  • African Fish Eagle
  • Ostrich
  • Pygmy Falcon

Silale Swamps Wildlife Magnet

The vast Silale Swamps in the southern region act as a giant green sponge. Holding water year-round, these swamps attract hundreds of elephants, wallowing buffaloes, wild dogs, and countless bird species during hot dry months.

Cultural Connections & Safari Experience

Living Around the Park Boundaries

The areas surrounding Tarangire National Park are inhabited by the Maasai and Barabaig pastoral communities. Visitors can combine game drives with authentic cultural village visits to learn about traditional livestock herding, ancient medicine, and indigenous customs.

Giant Baobab Trees (The Tree of Life)

Tarangire is iconic for its gigantic Baobab trees (Adansonia digitata). These trees store thousands of liters of water inside their massive trunks, providing vital moisture and bark nutrients to elephants during dry spells.
