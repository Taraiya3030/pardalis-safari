Mount Meru, which is located within the Arusha National Park, overlooking Arusha town, is the second highest mountain in Tanzania after Kilimanjaro. Standing proudly at 4,566 meters (14,980 feet) above sea level, Mount Meru is an exciting mountain to climb with breathtaking landscapes, rich biodiversity, and relatively fewer crowds.

Parts of the climb along the saddle are very steep but the views of Kilimanjaro 80km to the northeast and of the cliff face from the summit down to Meru Crater are spectacular. One does not however need technical equipment for the climb.

Mount Meru is usually scaled in 3 days / 2 nights, but since descending from summit takes about 7 hours, some climbers opt for an extra night at Miriakamba Hut which will make it a 4-day / 3-nights climb. Overnight accommodation is in alpine huts on your way up and down.

Mount Meru – Overview & History

Introduction

Mount Meru is a dormant stratovolcano that last erupted around 1910. Thousands of years ago, a massive volcanic explosion caused the eastern side of the mountain to collapse, creating a spectacular horseshoe-shaped crater that visitors can still observe today. The mountain's fertile volcanic soils support lush forests, diverse wildlife, and unique vegetation, making it one of Tanzania's ecological treasures.

Geography and Geological Formation

Mount Meru is situated in northern Tanzania within the Great Rift Valley region. One of its most striking characteristics is the enormous collapsed eastern crater wall with steep cliffs reaching nearly 1,500 meters high. Inside the crater lies an ash cone that formed after the major eruption. The summit, known as Socialist Peak, offers panoramic views across northern Tanzania, including the snow-capped peak of Mount Kilimanjaro on clear mornings.

Climate and Vegetation

Mount Meru experiences several climatic zones due to changes in altitude. The lower slopes receive abundant rainfall throughout the year, supporting dense tropical forests. At higher elevations, the vegetation gradually changes into montane forests and heathland dominated by giant heather. Above approximately 3,500 meters, the landscape becomes alpine desert with sparse vegetation adapted to harsh conditions.

Wildlife of Mount Meru

One of the greatest attractions of Mount Meru is the incredible wildlife found within Arusha National Park. Common mammals include giraffes, buffaloes, zebras, warthogs, bushbucks, waterbucks, and elephants. Visitors may also see blue monkeys, black-and-white colobus monkeys, baboons, and occasionally leopards. Because buffaloes are common on the lower slopes, all trekking groups are accompanied by armed park rangers to ensure visitors' safety.

Standard Trekking Itinerary

Day 1: Momela Gate to Miriakamba Hut

Morning transfer to Momela Gate (1500m) to start the climb, 3 hours walk gets you to Miriakamba Hut. Afternoon explore the Meru Crater. Overnight at Miriakamba Hut.

Day 2: Miriakamba Hut to Saddle Hut

Climb to Saddle Hut which will take about three hours along the steep Saddle walls. Explore Little Meru (3820m), a 1 ½ hour climb in the afternoon. Overnight at Saddle Hut.

Day 3: Summit Day & Descent

Early morning ascend to the summit of Mount Meru (4566m) which will take up to 5 hours and then return to Miriakamba Hut for overnight OR descend to Momela Gate (7 hours).

Cultural & Tourism Importance

Mount Meru holds cultural importance for several ethnic communities living around its slopes, including the Meru people and the Maasai. Although Mt Meru is usually climbed from the western side at Olkokola, it can be climbed via Little Meru. The best time to climb the mountain is between October and February, although there may be some rain in November.
