Zanzibar Archipelago

The Exotic Spice Island of the Indian Ocean

Zanzibar is a world-renowned tropical paradise located in the Indian Ocean, just 25 to 50 kilometers off the mainland coast of Tanzania. Comprising two main islands—Unguja (commonly referred to as Zanzibar) and Pemba—alongside numerous smaller coral islets, Zanzibar is a magical blend of pristine nature, vibrant Swahili culture, rich Arabian history, and turquoise coastal luxury.

Famous worldwide for its powdery white-sand beaches, coconut palm groves, crystal-clear warm waters, and thriving coral reefs, Zanzibar serves as the ultimate relaxation destination after an adventurous mainland wildlife safari in Serengeti or Ngorongoro Crater. Beyond its idyllic beaches, the archipelago possesses a rich history as a major trading hub for spices, silk, and gold across Africa, Arabia, Persia, and India.

Top Coastal Destinations & Beaches

Zanzibar’s coastline features distinct beach regions, each offering unique atmospheres, tidal conditions, and water sports activities.

Nungwi & Kendwa (Northern Coast)

Located on the northern tip of Unguja, Nungwi and Kendwa are famous for having virtually non-tidal beaches, allowing for uninterrupted all-day swimming and snorkeling. Kendwa is renowned for its wide golden stretches and vivid orange sunsets, while Nungwi offers lively beach bars, local dhow building yards, and vibrant nightlife.

Paje & Jambiani (South-East Coast)

Paje is the kite-surfing capital of East Africa, attracting water sport enthusiasts from across the world due to its shallow lagoons and steady trade winds. Neighboring Jambiani provides an authentic Swahili village experience with traditional seaweed farming and peaceful white sand stretches.

Matemwe & Mnemba Atoll (North-East Coast)

Matemwe faces the famous Mnemba Atoll marine reserve. It features quiet, secluded beaches overlooking translucent, shallow waters. It is the premier base for scuba diving and marine conservation excursions.

Culture, History & Excursions

Stone Town UNESCO World Heritage Site

Stone Town is the historical heart of Zanzibar. Wander through narrow labyrinthine alleys lined with intricately carved brass-studded Omani doors, visit the Sultan's Palace (Beit-al-Ajaib), explore the Old Fort, and shop for handcrafted souvenirs at the bustling Darajani Market.

Organic Spice Farm Tours

Discover why Zanzibar earned its nickname as the "Spice Island." Guided tours through local organic plantations allow visitors to smell, taste, and pick fresh spices straight from the tree:

  • Cloves
  • Nutmeg
  • Cinnamon
  • Cardamom
  • Black Pepper
  • Vanilla
  • Lemongrass
  • Ginger
  • Turmeric

Jozani Chwaka Bay National Park

Zanzibar's only national park protects dense mahogany groundwater forests and coastal mangrove swamps. It is the exclusive home of the endangered Red Colobus Monkey, a rare primate species found nowhere else on Earth.

Prison Island (Changuu) & Giant Tortoises

A short dhow boat ride from Stone Town lies Prison Island, home to a sanctuary of giant Aldabra tortoises—some of which are over 150 years old! The island offers excellent reef snorkeling and historical tours.

Ocean Adventures & Marine Life

Unforgettable Marine Experiences

The warm waters surrounding Zanzibar are teeming with tropical marine life, colorful coral reefs, and pods of wild dolphins:

  • Safari Blue Dhow Cruises
  • Snorkeling Mnemba Coral Gardens
  • Scuba Diving Deep Wall Reefs
  • Sunset Dhow Sailing
  • Kite Surfing in Paje
  • Kuza Cave Swimming
  • Kizimkazi Dolphin Watching

Best Time to Visit Zanzibar

Seasonal Travel Guide

June to October: Dry, cool, and sunny season with pleasant sea breezes—ideal for beach holidays and safaris.
December to February: Warm summer months with tropical temperatures, ideal for diving and deep-sea activities.
March to May: Long monsoon rains season; perfect for quiet escapes and discounted resort stays.
